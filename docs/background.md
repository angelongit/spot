## background ##

The project started as a solution to a problem I’ve had in the gym for a while. When lifting, I have a hard time accurately counting the number of repetitions I am performing due to focusing on the actual movement. This can become a problem when I’m trying to measure progress, so I started thinking about possible solutions.

I tried using my fitness watch’s workout feature, but it provided inaccurate results. The nature of wearables makes it difficult for them to count accurately, as they focus on wrist/arm movement and cannot ‘see’ the weight move. I decided that a wearable could potentially repeat the same problems I had already experienced.

This is when the idea to create a mountable device that does the counting for me was born. At first, the device was purely meant to count reps, but this didn’t seem worth the trouble as I could just record the set or get used to counting. To justify the project, I began thinking about other problems I have in the gym.

I proposed the idea to a couple of members of my organizations, and they agreed that it would be a good feature as they struggle with keeping form in check, especially later in the workout. After asking around, I landed on four core issue points to build the project around: Form Checking, Rep Timing, Rep Uniformity and Automatic Rest Detection.

After researching similar products to perform a ‘competitor analysis’, I found similar products that provided some features that I wanted. While the products could potentially be seen as competitors, none of them combined all the features in one device.

Rogo, a device created at my school Texas A&M, was a similar product but was vague about its metrics, claiming that it uses ‘AI’ to track workouts. Additionally, its snap-on nature can get in the way during certain exercises, or flat out not work. This flaw inspired my idea to create swappable mounts for my device.

Another competitor, RepOne, provided many of the features I planned on incorporating in the project. However, this product is aimed at coaches, pricing out average consumers at a whopping $449. My product is aimed at the general gym-goer and will focus on helping them standardize their form with easy-to-understand metrics and feedback.

I wanted to create the project myself so I could understand the architecture behind this type of device, and fix problems that I have personally experienced at the gym. Additionally, understanding the mechanics behind the device allows me to add features and understand my own workouts more.

Tracking your progress in the gym is important, and this extends beyond just the number of reps performed. I wanted to create a device that keeps me accountable and informed, not just for the number of reps but how those reps are performed. My device, Spot, will track metrics based on four core facets: position, acceleration, rotation, and velocity. From these metrics, the device will be able to count reps, measure tempo, time rest, and check form.

The goal for the initial prototype is to count rep and rest times correctly against video, and more accurately than a fitness watch. You shouldn’t have to worry about numbers in the gym; let Spot do the heavy lifting for you.
